### 1.3 Monthly perspective — not take-home

- ₹24,00,000 **annual gross salary** ÷ 12 = **₹2,00,000 per month gross**, *not* ₹2 lakh credited to the bank.
- ₹25,00,000 annual gross salary ÷ 12 = **₹2,08,333 per month gross**, *not* take-home.
- Actual monthly credit = payable monthly salary **minus** employee PF (if applicable), professional tax, income tax withholding and other deductions; sometimes compensation components are not paid monthly.
- A “₹25L fixed CTC” may include employer-side costs or retirement provisions, depending on the company's written definition. Demand a **gross cash salary** number in addition to total CTC.

### 1.4 Three internal negotiation levels

1. **Opening ask:** ₹26–27L annual fixed gross cash; emphasize flexibility on the *overall package structure*, not willingness to swap fixed for uncertain variable pay.
2. **Target close:** ₹24–25L annual fixed gross cash, excluding variable pay, stock and joining bonus.
3. **Private walk-away consideration:** Decide privately how much lower you would ever consider for an exceptionally good role, promotion path or documented near-term correction. You do **not** have to accept below your target. No tactic can force HR to exceed its approved budget.

I'd be happy to review the full structure, but my priority is the guaranteed fixed component.”

**If you prefer not to start at 26–27:**

> “For me, a suitable outcome would be **₹24–25 lakh of guaranteed fixed annual pay**, with variable compensation and any sign-on amount considered separately. Could you share the approved range and compensation structure for this role?”

A higher anchor may improve negotiating room but also risks appearing outside the role's budget. Choose the version you can defend naturally.

## 2. How to explain and defend your ₹24–25L fixed expectation

### 2.1 Your business case — more persuasive than percentage mathematics

Your documented experience provides concrete evidence:

- **Distributed Oracle Coherence cache:** Nexus lookup approximately **956 ms → 2 ms on cache hits**, reducing repeat remote calls and improving a 20-booking test from approximately **70 s → 19 s**.
- **Parallel booking-group processing:** Large **1,710-group** run approximately **439.2 s → 186.7 s**, a **57.49%** reduction, using a bounded, configurable pool and independent cloned contexts.
- **Trade archival:** Moved **18.5 million trades**, approximately **340 GB**, improving average retrieval from **45 s → 8 s** while preserving data integrity.
- **Bulk instrument expiry:** Converted constrained synchronous/manual work into job-tracked parallel processing; **1,000 instruments: 1 hour → about 2 minutes**.
- **Financial controls and production ownership:** Worked on counterparty eligibility, STP integration, cross-system incidents, regression testing and modernization.

**What these points prove:** You understand performance **and** correctness in regulated financial workflows; you are not merely describing CRUD APIs. You need not disclose confidential business data or internal architecture diagrams in negotiation.

### 2.2 Your 30-second value statement

> “My expectation is based on the engineering value and responsibility I can bring to the role. I've delivered measurable improvements in distributed caching, multithreaded processing and large-scale data maintenance, and I have experience handling production-critical financial workflows. I would like the compensation to reflect both the scope of the role and this capability rather than being determined only by a percentage increase on my current salary.”

### 2.3 Why fixed salary matters to you

> “I prefer the primary compensation to be predictable because fixed salary is what I can reliably plan around. I'm happy to consider variable pay, equity or a joining bonus as additional upside, but I wouldn't want them to replace the fixed compensation we are discussing.”

### 2.4 Never confuse the following terms

| Term | What you need to verify |
|---|---|
| **Annual fixed gross cash salary** | Guaranteed yearly salary paid through payroll, before employee deductions/tax; excludes one-time and performance-contingent pay. |
| **Fixed CTC** | May include employer PF, gratuity provisions and other employer costs. Ask what's included. |
| **Total CTC** | Could contain fixed salary, target/max bonus, equity valuations, benefits, and employer contributions. A headline figure may not translate into cash. |
| **Variable pay** | Target/maximum payout, performance conditions, eligibility date, past payouts, proration and payout schedule. |
| **Joining bonus** | One-off taxable income with possible clawback; not a substitute for recurring fixed salary. |
| **RSUs/ESOPs/stocks** | Equity type, vesting schedule, liquidity and valuation determine true value. |
| **Monthly in-hand** | Payroll bank credit after deductions and withholding; depends on personal tax setup. |

**Precise wording to use:** “When I say ₹25 lakh fixed, I mean **annual guaranteed gross cash salary excluding employer PF/gratuity and any variable or one-time components**. Could you confirm the company's definition and provide the gross monthly breakup?”

## 3. The exact questions to ask HR: 15 from your screenshots

These preserve the 15 topics in the uploaded images, with wording tightened for an HR offer discussion. **Ask for written confirmation** and don't ask all 15 during the first five minutes of an initial compensation screening call. Ask the highest-value questions when a preliminary package is available, then email the remaining ones as an offer-clarification checklist.

### 1. Exact fixed compensation

**Ask exactly:** “What is my **guaranteed annual fixed gross cash salary**, excluding variable pay, bonuses, equity, gratuity provision and employer PF? Could you also state the annual **fixed CTC** separately?”

**Why:** Prevents a company-cost figure from being mistaken for paid salary.

**If HR says ‘₹25L fixed CTC’:** “What portion of that ₹25L will be annual gross salary actually paid via payroll? Please show employer PF and gratuity as separate lines.”

### 2. Complete written CTC breakup

**Ask exactly:** “Could you share the complete compensation breakup **in writing**, including basic, HRA, special allowances, gross monthly salary, employer and employee PF, gratuity provision, insurance, variable pay, equity and any one-time components?”

**Follow-up:** “Which amounts are guaranteed, which are target-based, which are maximum, and which are not cash?”

### 3. Percentage and treatment of variable pay

**Ask exactly:** “What percentage of the quoted CTC is variable? Does the CTC include **target variable** or the **maximum possible variable**?”

**Follow-up:** “Can you show total guaranteed compensation separately from total compensation at target bonus and maximum bonus?”

### 4. Variable payout formula and frequency

**Ask exactly:** “How is variable pay calculated, what are the individual, team and company performance weightages, and is it paid monthly, quarterly or annually?”

**Follow-up:** “Is the first year's variable prorated, is there a minimum tenure cut-off, and must I be employed on the payout date?”

### 5. Actual historical bonus payouts

**Ask exactly:** “For employees in this role/grade, what proportion received **100% of target variable** in the most recent completed payout cycle? If individual data isn't shareable, can you share the typical payout range or a published policy?”

**Why:** ‘Up to 15%’ does not mean everyone receives 15%.

### 6. Approximate monthly take-home

**Ask exactly:** “Based on the proposed breakup, can payroll provide an **indicative monthly net-pay illustration** after employee PF, professional tax and typical TDS, specifying the tax-regime assumptions?”

**Follow-up:** “Please separately show gross monthly salary before tax and net monthly salary after estimated deductions. I understand actual tax depends on my declarations.”

### 7. PF and gratuity within CTC

**Ask exactly:** “Does the quoted CTC include **employer PF** and **gratuity provision**? Are these counted within ‘fixed compensation’ or shown separately? What PF wage base or applicable cap does your payroll follow?”

**Why:** Employer costs do not generally increase the monthly salary you receive in your bank account.

### 8. Joining bonus and clawback

**Ask exactly:** “Is there a joining or sign-on bonus? If so, when is it paid, is it taxable, and is there a repayment/clawback clause if I leave within 6, 12, 18 or 24 months?”

**Follow-up:** “Is repayment gross or net of tax, and does any amount become non-repayable after a specified period?”

### 9. Retention bonus

**Ask exactly:** “Is any retention or deferred bonus included in the quoted CTC? When would I become eligible, and what conditions apply to receiving or retaining it?”

### 10. First appraisal and salary revision

**Ask exactly:** “When is the regular appraisal cycle, and when would I first be eligible for a performance review and salary revision after joining?”

### 11. Next appraisal eligibility for the proposed joining date

**Ask exactly:** “If my date of joining is **[confirmed date]**, would I qualify for the **next** appraisal cycle? What is the minimum-tenure or cut-off rule, and is any revision prorated?”

**Why:** Joining just after the cut-off may delay the first revision considerably.

### 12. ESOPs / RSUs / equity

**Ask exactly:** “Does the offered CTC include ESOPs, RSUs or other equity? If yes, what is the grant type, valuation method, vesting schedule, cliff, exercise price if applicable, and how much of the quoted annual CTC represents equity?”

**Follow-up:** “Is there an established liquidity/exit mechanism, and what happens to unvested awards when employment ends?”

### 13. Benefits included versus over and above CTC

**Ask exactly:** “Which benefits are costed within my CTC and which are offered **over and above** it—for example, health insurance, dependent coverage, meal benefits, internet, transport/cab support, wellness, learning budget and reimbursements?”

### 14. Notice period during and after probation

**Ask exactly:** “What is the notice period during probation and after confirmation? Is notice-period buyout permitted, who approves it, and are there restrictions?”

### 15. Bond / service agreement / recovery clauses

**Ask exactly:** “Are there any minimum-service obligations, employment bonds, training recovery clauses, relocation repayment conditions, non-compete or non-solicitation provisions, or other financial liabilities I should know about before accepting?”

**Follow-up:** “Please share the exact contractual wording, not just a verbal assurance.”

## 4. Additional questions you should ask before accepting

The screenshots cover many important issues. These additional questions catch common compensation-structure and offer-stage surprises.

### A. Salary definition and payment mechanics

16. “Does **fixed salary** include any guaranteed annual payment that is **not** paid monthly, such as a 13th-month salary or fixed annual allowance?”
17. “What is the **annual gross cash salary** and **monthly gross payroll salary**, separately from total CTC?”
18. “What are the **employee** and **employer** PF amounts in rupees per month? Are contributions based on full eligible wages or a cap?”
19. “Are there any salary deductions beyond income tax, employee PF and professional tax, such as insurance premium sharing or employee welfare contributions?”
20. “If there is a salary revision during onboarding, is the **revised amount** reflected in the original written offer or a signed addendum?”
21. “When is salary credited, and is the first month's payment prorated based on joining date?”

### B. Bonus, stock, and joining date

22. “What is the **guaranteed first-year compensation** if I join mid-year and receive zero performance bonus?”
23. “Will I be eligible for the upcoming bonus cycle? What is the cut-off date, and how is the first year's payment prorated?”
24. “If I forgo a scheduled compensation review or equity vesting at Barclays to join, is there any **make-whole or sign-on** support?”
25. “If equity is awarded, does the company issue a **separate grant agreement** and are there annual refresh grants?”
26. “If an agreed joining bonus is recovered on resignation, how is the associated tax accounted for?”

### C. Role scope and career progression

27. “What **grade/level** will appear in my offer letter, and how does it map to software developer or senior developer responsibilities?”
28. “What is the **approved pay range for this grade**, and is there room for an exception if the fixed salary is below my expectation?”
29. “What specific outcomes would I need to achieve for a promotion or a meaningful salary revision, and when can that first be considered?”
30. “Is there any difference between my proposed **job title, actual responsibilities and salary grade**?”
31. “Who approves compensation exceptions—the recruiter, hiring manager, HR business partner or compensation committee?”

### D. Work arrangements and employment terms

32. “What is the expected work location, hybrid/on-site requirement and office attendance policy for my team?”
33. “Will the role require on-call rotations, weekend deployments or support outside regular working hours? Is there an allowance or compensatory leave policy?”
34. “Are there travel or relocation expectations, and which associated expenses are reimbursed?”
35. “What is the probation period, and are there any differences in compensation, insurance or bonus eligibility during probation?”
36. “What are the leave entitlements, carry-forward rules and leave-encashment policy?”
37. “What is the employment entity listed on the offer, and which policies apply to the location where I'll work?”

### E. Written-offer protections

38. “Is the offer contingent on background verification, reference checks, customer approvals or other conditions? What are the expected timelines?”
39. “Can the written offer confirm the **fixed gross salary, variable target, joining bonus, grade, location, joining date and notice period**?”
40. “Is there a date by which I must accept, and may I have time to review the compensation breakdown and employment agreement?”
41. “If we agree to a future salary review or guaranteed correction, can it be documented with a **specific date, amount and approval authority**?”
42. “Who should I contact for clarification if an element in the formal offer differs from our discussion?”

**How to prioritize:** In the first HR call, focus on **1, 2, 3, 7, 10–11, 27–28**; during final offer review, work through the rest. Don't turn the first friendly recruiter call into an interrogation.

## 5. HR objections and your word-for-word responses

**How to use this section:** Practice the response in quotes, then stop talking. Let the recruiter respond. Do not argue for five minutes, apologize for your number, or invent a competing offer. HR's role is often to work inside a budget and approved internal-equity bands; yours is to help them make a credible business case for your compensation.

### A. Opening and anchoring

#### Scenario 1 — “What is your salary expectation?”

**Say:**

> “For the scope we've discussed, I'm looking at **₹26–27 lakh in annual guaranteed fixed gross salary**. I'm open to understanding the overall package, but fixed compensation is my priority. Could you share the approved range for this role?”

**Why it works:** Anchors above your ₹24–25L goal and asks for budget context. You can instead open at ₹24–25L if you prefer a lower-risk anchor.

#### Scenario 2 — “What is your current CTC?”

**Say:**

> “My current **annual fixed compensation is ₹16.5 lakh**. I also receive an annual stock component valued at approximately **₹50,000**, so those are separate pieces. The precise comparison should be between fixed salary and fixed salary, rather than using total CTC interchangeably.”

**Guardrail:** If your salary statement legally defines ₹16.5L as *fixed CTC including employer PF*, disclose that accurately instead of calling it gross cash pay. Correct the terminology once the breakdown is in front of you.

#### Scenario 3 — “Can you share payslips or current compensation documents?”

**Say:**

> “Yes, I can provide the appropriate documents through your secure HR process at the relevant stage, after any sensitive nonessential details are handled according to policy. Please let me know exactly which documents you need.”

**Guardrail:** Provide truthful documentation. Do not alter documents, inflate salary figures, or share sensitive employer/customer information unnecessarily.

#### Scenario 4 — “Why do you expect such a big hike when you're at ₹16.5L?”

**Say:**

> “I understand it looks significant as a percentage of my current fixed pay. I see this change as a move into a new role with broader engineering expectations, rather than an annual increment. I've delivered measurable improvements in caching, concurrency and financial transaction workflows. I'm also close to my normal compensation cycle. I'd like to evaluate the salary against the role's responsibility and expected contribution, not only my historical pay.”

#### Scenario 5 — “Our policy allows only a 30% increase on current salary.”

**Say:**

> “I understand you may have a guideline. Is it an absolute policy for this grade, or is there an exception process based on interview assessment and role fit? At 30% on my current fixed, the outcome would be **₹21.45L**, below what I'm targeting. Could we explore an approval closer to **₹24–25L fixed**?”

**Follow-up if no exceptions:** “Can we review the role's grade, range and the highest guaranteed fixed salary actually available?” Do not argue about an internally mandatory ceiling you cannot change.

#### Scenario 6 — “₹26–27L is outside our budget.”

**Say:**

> “Thank you for clarifying. I gave that as my initial expectation. If there's room to structure the offer at **₹24–25L guaranteed fixed**, I would be happy to seriously consider it. What is the maximum fixed salary approved for this role?”

**Guardrail:** This is a controlled concession from your opening anchor, not a retreat into bonus-heavy CTC.

#### Scenario 7 — “Our maximum is ₹22L fixed. Take it or leave it.”

**Say:**

> “I appreciate the transparency and remain interested in the position. However, ₹22L fixed is meaningfully below my target, particularly given the timing of my current review cycle. Is that the absolute maximum even with hiring-manager or compensation-team approval? If it is, I may need to pause rather than commit to compensation that doesn't meet my requirements.”

**Guardrail:** An offer can genuinely be below your requirement. It's legitimate to decline rather than accept and resent the decision later.

#### Scenario 8 — “Other candidates are accepting less.”

**Say:**

> “I understand there may be other candidates with different compensation expectations. My request reflects the value I believe I can contribute and what would make the transition worthwhile for me. If the team sees a strong fit, I'd appreciate exploring a fixed package around **₹24–25L**.”

#### Scenario 9 — “The market doesn't pay ₹25L for three years of experience.”

**Say:**

> “I understand that experience is one factor in leveling. My request also reflects my project ownership and measurable delivery in production financial systems. I'm happy to align the discussion to the responsibilities and salary band for this specific role. Could you share the grade and fixed salary range?”

**Guardrail:** Don't falsely claim external salary surveys or competing companies' packages.

#### Scenario 10 — “We can't share the salary band.”

**Say:**

> “I understand. Could you at least confirm whether **₹24–25L guaranteed fixed** is feasible for this position, and what approvals would be required? That would help both of us avoid spending time on a structure that won't work.”

#### Scenario 11 — “What is your absolute minimum?”

**Say:**

> “Rather than focus on a minimum immediately, I'd prefer to understand the best package the company can offer for this role. The range I'm aiming to close around is **₹24–25L fixed**, depending on the exact structure and responsibilities.”

**Guardrail:** Your real walk-away number is private. Don't make up a fake ‘hard floor’ that you are not prepared to enforce.

#### Scenario 12 — “Are you willing to compromise? Give me one final number.”

**Say:**

> “If it helps to move quickly, **₹25L annual guaranteed fixed gross salary**, with any variable or sign-on components on top, is a package I would be comfortable seriously evaluating. Could you seek approval for that structure?”

**Note:** Say “seriously evaluating,” not an unconditional promise to join; first review grade, contract, benefits and other conditions.

#### Scenario 13 — “We can offer ₹24L. Can you accept today?”

**Say:**

> “₹24L fixed is within the range I was targeting, assuming it means annual **gross guaranteed salary**, separate from employer PF, gratuity and bonus. Please share the complete written breakdown and terms so I can review them promptly. I wouldn't want to commit before confirming those details.”

### B. Packaging and salary-structure tactics

**Say:**

> “I understand that may be how your internal fixed CTC is defined. For a like-for-like comparison, could you show the **annual gross cash salary paid through payroll** separately? My target is ₹24–25L in that guaranteed gross salary, with employer-side costs stated in addition.”

**Important:** You can request that structure but cannot force the company to use your definition. The written, rupee-level breakup is what matters.

#### Scenario 16 — “We can do ₹22L fixed + ₹3L variable + ₹2L joining bonus = ₹27L CTC.”

**Say:**

> “That's a generous-looking first-year package, and I appreciate the effort. My concern is that **₹22L is the recurring fixed salary**; the variable and joining bonus may not repeat or fully pay out. Could we prioritize getting the annual fixed closer to **₹24–25L** and then discuss any additional bonus separately?”

#### Scenario 17 — “We can pay ₹23L fixed plus a ₹2L signing bonus.”

**Say:**

> “I appreciate the sign-on support. Since a signing bonus is one-time and may have repayment conditions, I'd still prefer to reach **₹24–25L in recurring fixed salary**. Is it possible to move **₹1–2L** of that proposed value into the fixed component?”

**Say:**

> “The first year would be stronger, but from year two onward my guaranteed compensation would remain ₹22L. I'm evaluating a longer-term move. Would you consider ₹24L fixed with a smaller sign-on, or a documented guaranteed fixed adjustment at a defined date?”

**Say:**

> “If it is contractually guaranteed, can we explicitly state the **minimum payable amount**, timing, proration conditions and any eligibility or resignation restrictions in the offer? Otherwise, I should treat it as variable upside, not fixed compensation.”

#### Scenario 20 — “Our annual bonus is very good—some employees get 150%.”

**Say:**

> “That's attractive upside. Could you share the **target** bonus, payout conditions and historical typical payout by grade? I would evaluate it separately from the guaranteed ₹24–25L fixed salary I'm targeting.”

#### Scenario 21 — “We provide ESOPs worth ₹3L, so you are already at ₹25L.”

**Say:**

> “I value equity as a long-term incentive. However, vesting, valuation and liquidity are different from guaranteed cash salary. I would prefer to compare the **₹24–25L fixed cash component** first and then consider ESOPs as additional upside.”

#### Scenario 22 — “The employer PF and gratuity are also your money; why exclude them?”

**Say:**

> “I agree retirement benefits have value. My concern is specifically about predictable salary paid through payroll, so I'd like the gross cash salary and employer-side benefits shown separately. I'm not saying the benefits have zero value—only that they shouldn't be confused with current monthly compensation.”

#### Scenario 23 — “₹24L is fixed, but ₹3L is a guaranteed annual allowance paid once a year.”

**Say:**

> “Could we check whether that allowance is **contractually guaranteed**, unconditional, payable even if I join mid-year, and protected from performance or resignation restrictions? If it is genuinely guaranteed recurring salary, I'm willing to evaluate it as part of fixed cash, but I'd want the payout schedule clearly documented.”

#### Scenario 24 — “We have a retention bonus if you stay two years.”

**Say:**

> “I appreciate the retention incentive, but it is deferred and conditional. I'd prefer to get the recurring fixed salary aligned first, then evaluate the retention payment on its own terms.”

#### Scenario 25 — “We cannot change fixed but can offer better insurance and benefits.”

**Say:**

> “Those benefits are helpful, and I would like to understand them. They don't fully bridge the difference in guaranteed fixed compensation, though. If the fixed salary cannot move, could you clarify the grade, timing of the first guaranteed review, or whether there is an exception route?”

### C. Your February increment and opportunity cost

#### Scenario 26 — “Your February increment hasn't happened, so we can't count it.”

**Say:**

> “That's fair. **₹16.5L is my current actual fixed salary**, and I don't want to misrepresent that. The February review is an expected opportunity cost of moving now, not an amount I'm claiming to already receive. My request is ultimately based on the new role's value, scope and the overall decision to transition.”

**This is the most defensible response.**

#### Scenario 27 — “What proof do you have of the 12–15% February increment?”

**Say:**

> “The review outcome is not yet reflected in an official revised salary letter, so I wouldn't present it as confirmed compensation. I'm referring to my expectation for the coming cycle. I'm happy to provide my actual current salary documents and discuss the new role's compensation independently.”

**If you actually have a confirmed written letter:** You can instead disclose the approved figure and provide it through HR's secure channel, if you choose.

#### Scenario 28 — “Everyone expects a raise; that shouldn't affect our offer.”

**Say:**

> “I understand. I mention it only because timing matters when evaluating a move. Even without that point, I believe the fixed range I'm discussing is appropriate for the responsibilities and engineering contribution I can bring.”

#### Scenario 29 — “Then stay at Barclays until February and join later.”

**Say:**

> “I'm open to discussing a mutually suitable joining date, depending on the project need and formal notice period. My main interest is a well-aligned long-term role. If the team needs an earlier joining date, I'd appreciate considering that opportunity cost in the compensation arrangement.”

**Do not promise a start date before checking notice and obligations.**

**Say:**

### 7.4 How to make a decision

Prioritize, in roughly this order:

1. **Role/technical growth and manager quality.** The next role should improve your career trajectory, not simply your first-year CTC.
2. **Recurring guaranteed cash pay.** This is your explicitly stated financial priority.
3. **Written contract and employee obligations.** Look for restrictive clauses, repayment obligations and probation terms.
4. **Appraisal timing and advancement.** A higher salary with a long freeze may be less attractive than it appears.
5. **Variable, equity, joining bonus, benefits and work-life implications.** Treat each as a distinct component.

**Practical acceptance rule:** If HR offers **₹24–25L in annual gross fixed cash** plus a reasonable job/contract structure, that satisfies your stated compensation target. Don't automatically reject because a more ambitious opening anchor wasn't achieved.

## 8. The upcoming February increment: strong but truthful framing

### 8.1 The arithmetic

Your current **₹16.50L fixed** would be:

- At **12%:** ₹16.50L + ₹1.98L = **₹18.48L fixed**.
- At **15%:** ₹16.50L + ₹2.475L = **₹18.975L fixed** (about ₹18.98L).

Your **₹24–25L** target would then be about **26.5–35.3% above** those hypothetical post-review salaries, depending on which endpoints are compared. HR may not formally use a projected salary as the baseline; that's okay. The new-role value case must stand independently.

### 8.2 Best exact response

> “My current fixed salary is ₹16.5 lakh, with an additional stock component of around ₹50,000 annually. Our compensation review cycle is approaching in February, and I **expect** a revision of roughly 12–15%, although I haven't received a formal revised salary letter yet. Moving now means making a decision before that normal compensation event. I mention it for context, but my expectation for this role is principally driven by the responsibilities and the experience I bring. I'm looking to settle around **₹24–25 lakh guaranteed fixed annual salary**.”

### 8.3 If the increment really has been formally approved

If you later obtain a salary-revision letter or official confirmation specifying the **effective date and amount**, replace the word *expect* with a factual statement. Example:

> “My revised fixed salary of **₹[exact amount]** has been formally confirmed, effective **[date]**. I can share appropriate proof through the secure HR process. I'd like the offer comparison to take that confirmed revision into account.”

**Do not say** “The increments are 100% locked and I will definitely get 15%” without documentary support. An informal forecast can change.

### 8.4 How to explain the opportunity cost without threatening HR

> “The move involves giving up the certainty of continuing in a role where I'm established, taking on a new team's expectations, and potentially missing a near-term review. I'm open to the change because the role is attractive; I just want the overall proposition to justify that transition.”

Avoid “I will get 15% anyway so you owe me 15% plus another 30%.” That sounds mechanical and will be easy to challenge.

### 8.5 If you would forgo existing stock or bonus

You stated approximately **₹50,000 annual stock value**. Only if true, determine:

- Next vesting date and value that is actually **unvested and forfeited** on departure.
- Whether a current-year bonus has actually been earned and will be forfeited.
- Whether a February revised salary would apply only after a date by which you would already leave.

**Say:**

> “In addition to the fixed salary comparison, my timing may involve forfeiting **₹[verified value]** in unvested equity or bonus. Would the company consider a one-time sign-on arrangement to address that specific loss, while keeping the recurring fixed salary around ₹24–25L?”

Don't label the entire annual stock value a guaranteed forfeiture unless vesting records support it.

## 11. Negotiation mistakes and risk controls

### Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying “₹25L in hand” when you mean “₹25L annual fixed gross.”** The former can imply after-tax pay; clarify immediately.
2. **Negotiating only total CTC.** It can hide variable, noncash, retirement and one-time components.
3. **Using ₹17L as your fixed salary.** Your stated current fixed is ₹16.5L; the other ₹50k is stocks.
4. **Presenting the February 12–15% increment as already earned or approved.** Only say it is confirmed once documented.
5. **Inventing a competing offer, fabricated salary slips, or market salary benchmarks.** You don't need any of these to negotiate credibly.
6. **Bluffing a hard walk-away if you aren't prepared to leave the process.** HR may simply withdraw or maintain the offer.
7. **Criticizing Barclays' technology or people.** Frame the move around newer engineering challenges and growth.
8. **Negotiating the same ₹1L repeatedly after a credible final approval.** Ask clearly once or twice, then decide.
9. **Accepting an oral future-increment promise as guaranteed.** Get a written commitment or treat it as uncertain.
10. **Resigning before reviewing the offer and contingencies.** Background checks, approvals or documents may still matter.
11. **Confusing salary with career value.** Salary is important, but role content, manager, skill development and work culture matter too.
12. **Sharing proprietary work details or internal screenshots.** Discuss achievements at a high level and avoid confidential artifacts.
13. **Treating HR as an opponent.** They often need your evidence to advocate for an exception internally.

### Good phrases to practice

- “For clarity, are we discussing **gross fixed cash salary** or total CTC?”
- “I appreciate the work you've done to improve the offer.”
- “I'm looking for an agreement that makes sense for both sides.”
- “The **recurring guaranteed component** is my main priority.”
- “Could you confirm whether there's a compensation approval path?”
- “I understand the budget may be fixed; if so, I will make an informed decision.”
- “Would you please share the **written compensation breakup**?”
- “I wouldn't want to misstate my expected February review as an already confirmed revision.”

### Phrases to avoid

- “I'm worth this because I need a 60% hike.”
- “My current employer is bad and uses only old systems.”
- “I definitely have a 15% raise locked in,” unless formally confirmed.
- “I have a ₹30L competing offer,” unless you truly do.
- “Just tell me the highest you can go; I'll accept immediately,” before contract review.
- “Variable pay is worthless.” It has potential value, just not the same certainty as fixed pay.
- “I will never accept below ₹25L,” unless that really is your hard and enforceable decision.

### Reality check

No negotiation script can **guarantee** a ₹24–25L fixed outcome. HR may be constrained by role leveling, current internal parity, budget or an actual compensation ceiling. Your control is over how clearly you communicate value, separate guaranteed salary from headline CTC, avoid factual misstatements, document terms, and decide when an offer is not right for you.
